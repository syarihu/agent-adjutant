  zunda-related weapon, and she was later joined by
  a small companion named Zundamon, described as a
  fairy of zunda mochi. Zundamon became hugely
  popular through free voice-synthesis software and
  countless online videos. As a result, many young
  people across Japan now know the word "zunda" from
  a cartoon fairy before they have ever tasted the
  dish. It is an odd turn for a food once tied to
  ancestral offerings, but it fits the dish's long
  role as a carrier of Tohoku identity.

  Zunda mochi's history follows a common pattern for
  Japanese regional foods. It started as a
  practical, seasonal use of what grew locally,
  shaped by ritual and the farming calendar. It took
  on legends that tied it to famous historical
  figures, and its name changed through local
  dialect. In the modern era it was turned into a
  symbol that represents a whole region to the rest
  of the country. Under the shakes, souvenir boxes,
  and mascots, the core of the dish has stayed the
  same: young green beans, pounded by hand, spread
  over warm rice cakes at the end of summer.
