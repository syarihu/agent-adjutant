  Zunda depends on eating soybeans young. Edamame
  are soybeans picked while still green and soft,
  before they ripen into the dry, pale beans used
  for tofu, miso, and soy sauce. Eating them this
  way was common by the Edo period (1603 to 1868).
  Street vendors in Edo sold boiled edamame still on
  the branch, which is where the name, meaning
  roughly "branch beans," comes from. In the
  countryside, picking some of the crop green was a
  natural way to enjoy it in late summer, when the
  pods are full but the beans are still tender.

  That timing matters. Zunda is above all a dish of
  the Tohoku region, the northeastern part of
  Honshu, and especially of Miyagi Prefecture and
  its capital, Sendai. It is also made in Yamagata,
  Fukushima, and Iwate. In these areas edamame ripen
  in late summer, around the time of Obon. Making
  mochi as an offering for returning ancestors and
  covering it with a paste of freshly picked green
  beans tied the dish to the calendar of farm life.
