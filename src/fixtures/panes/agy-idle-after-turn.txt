  Barley tea, widely known as mugicha in Japan, boricha in Korea, and dàmàichá in China, is an
  East Asian roasted-grain infusion cherished for its deep, comforting flavor and refreshing
  qualities. Crafted simply by simmering or steeping roasted unhulled barley kernels in water,
  this golden-brown beverage delivers a distinctively toasted, nutty aroma with subtle earthy
  undertones and a mild, natural sweetness.

  Unlike traditional teas derived from the Camellia sinensis plant, barley tea is completely
  caffeine-free and contains virtually no sugar or calories. This makes it a wholesome, hydrating
  staple suitable for all ages at any hour of the day or night. Traditionally believed to aid
  digestion, relieve stress, and cool the body, its gentle roasted bitterness also balances rich
  foods, making it an ideal companion for everyday meals.

  During sweltering summer months, massive pitchers of iced barley tea reside in refrigerators
  across East Asia, serving as the quintessential antidote to humid heat. Conversely, during
  colder seasons, it is served steaming hot to provide soothing, aromatic warmth and relaxation.
  Whether brewed from whole roasted grains or steeped conveniently using tea bags, barley tea
  remains an enduring household favorite across generations, celebrated for its pure simplicity,
  rustic appeal, and universally restorative, comforting charm.
